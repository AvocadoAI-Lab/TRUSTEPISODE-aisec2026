\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{sloppypar}
\trustepisode's supported contribution is not superior episode-formation accuracy. It is a
canonicalized, single-writer reconstruction contract that preserves immutable revision history,
emits append-only terminal outcomes, evaluates all seven predefined AssemblyFailureRecord codes,
localizes evidence loss with the A--G taxonomy, and exposes a typed read-only boundary for
downstream AI. On the sealed cohort, EB4 shows a descriptive mean coverage increase from
\EbZeroCov{} to \TeCov{} relative to the legacy fixed-bin reference, but fair sliding builders do
not establish TrustEpisode superiority: EB4 matches EB2 on all 70 action-coverage pairs and trails
EB1/EB3 on one concurrent-benign M7 run ($\Delta=\EbOneActionDelta{}$, 95\% CI
$[\EbOneActionCILo{},\EbOneActionCIHi{}]$; not statistically resolved). Canonical replay is
byte-identical on \DetNRuns{} M1--M6 runs; raw arrival order requires re-canonicalization. Eight
downstream cards reject six invalid or unsealed mutations and accept a resealed allowed-field
control, so an unkeyed digest is not writer authentication. Once-run Qwen and locked Gemma audits
(\LlmPilotExact{}/20 and \GemmaPilotExact{}/20 fully exact) show inspectable model-dependent
failure, not general factuality. Advanced mechanisms remain mainly conformance-tested; fitted
scoring and writer authorization stay unevaluated. The result is a falsifiable evidence substrate
that narrows what an untrusted model may treat as case fact.
\end{sloppypar}

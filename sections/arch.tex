\section{TrustEpisode Method}
\label{sec:method}

\trustepisode converts EDR/NDR telemetry into deterministic candidate revisions and
version-scoped evidence records. Evidence Fabric and Health Monitor respectively own events
and source health; Episode Synthesis alone owns membership; Feature Extractor emits health-blind
$D,C,m$ and detector observations; Stateless Scorer emits $z$; Scope Resolver emits resolved
detector availability, $\kappa$, the calibration-group key, and \path{decision_eligible};
Calibrator emits nullable $P$; and Sufficiency Evaluator emits structured $U$. For each revision,
the AuditRecord Assembler emits exactly one terminal AssemblyOutcome: a canonical AuditRecord
when its join succeeds, or an immutable AssemblyFailureRecord when it cannot safely assemble one.
Ground truth is joined only after inference. The method estimates malicious activity inside a
frozen EDR/NDR candidate; it does not estimate enterprise risk, impact, response priority, or
unobservable attacks. Figure~\ref{fig:method-architecture} shows these boundaries.

\subsection{Problem Scope, Output Contract, and Ownership}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

Let $\mathcal C$ contain every emitted EpisodeRevision and define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sup}}=
\{\epsilon^{(r)}\in\mathcal C:
\kappa_{\epsilon^{(r)}}\in\{\kappa_G,\kappa_g\}\},
\label{eq:supported-population}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_G$, $\kappa_g$, and $\kappa_\varnothing$ serialize as
\path{supported_global}, \path{supported_group}, and \path{out_of_support}. The offline
target $Y_{\epsilon^{(r)}}\in\{0,1\}$ is revision specific: evidence first accepted in $r+1$
cannot relabel $r$. Positive and negative revisions form the primary supervised cohort; mixed
and ambiguous revisions enter only the registered sensitivity analysis.

When defined, the candidate-conditioned output is
\begin{equation}
P_{\epsilon^{(r)}}=\Pr(Y_{\epsilon^{(r)}}=1\mid
\epsilon^{(r)},v_{\mathrm{episode}},v_{\mathrm{feature}},
v_{\mathrm{model}},v_{\mathrm{cal}}).
\label{eq:scoped-probability}
\end{equation}
It is not the unconditional probability that an organization is under attack. Online schemas
are label blind and reject scenario, split, marker, window, and label fields. Collector
identity and health messages are authenticated; activity outside observable EDR/NDR traces is
outside the claim.

forbids downstream components from silently taking over that ownership.

\subsection{Evidence, Health, and Detector Availability}
\label{sec:evidence-fabric}

Evidence Fabric maps each source record to a NormalizedEvent with stable ID, event and ingest
times, source instance, canonical subject/object/action, immutable raw pointer, and root
dependency group. Forwarded copies retain their root and cannot add evidence. OCSF v1.8.0 is
only a field-alignment target~\cite{ocsf2026}. Health Monitor independently emits authenticated
SourceCoverage intervals from heartbeat, lag, parser, and transport observations; silence is
never interpreted as failure. Each manifest fixes a 30 s heartbeat period: \texttt{healthy}
requires no missed heartbeat, lag $\le120$ s, valid parser, and live transport;
\texttt{degraded} means one missed heartbeat or $120<$ lag $\le600$ s while transport remains
live; \texttt{down} requires an authenticated collector/transport stop or two consecutive missed
heartbeats; missing or conflicting intervals yield \texttt{unknown}.

For $s\in\mathcal S^\star=\{\mathrm{EDR},\mathrm{NDR}\}$,
\begin{equation}
m_{\epsilon,s}=\mathbf{1}[\epsilon\text{ contains an observed event from }s].
\label{eq:source-mask}
\end{equation}
Feature Extractor is the sole producer of $m$ and health-independent observation
$o^{det}_{\epsilon,s}\in\{\texttt{detected},\texttt{examined\_none},
\texttt{not\_examined}\}$. It reads EpisodeRevision and detector outputs, but never
SourceCoverage. Scope Resolver is the sole producer of resolved availability $a^{det}$ and
selects coverage at $t_{sup}=\texttt{EpisodeRevision.event\_time\_end}$, not the later
watermark or assembly wall time. It requires exactly one content-distinct covering interval per
expected source instance and applies, in order: missing or conflicting intervals $\mapsto\texttt{unknown}$;
unexpected/down source $\mapsto\texttt{collector\_unavailable}$; invalid parser
$\mapsto\texttt{parser\_invalid}$; unknown health or \texttt{not\_examined}
$\mapsto\texttt{unknown}$; otherwise \texttt{detected}$\mapsto\texttt{available}$ and
\texttt{examined\_none}$\mapsto\texttt{no\_detection}$. Thus event silence never becomes
health. Finally, $q_{\epsilon,s}=1$ requires lineage-deduplicated detector support tied to a core
entity or edge. Scope Resolver consumes $(m,o^{det},h)$, eligible-detector count $n$, and exact
versions and alone emits SupportDecision. It contains $a^{det},\kappa,g,e,n$, an eligibility
reason, \path{coverage_ref_ids}, and \path{health_snapshot_digest}; $e$ is
\path{decision_eligible}. Before calibration resolution, $n=0$ deterministically yields
$\kappa=\kappa_\varnothing$, $e=0$, reason \path{no_eligible_detector_value}, and therefore
JSON-null $P$, even when both resolved states are \path{no_detection}. Otherwise $e=1$ iff all
support predicates pass and $\kappa\in\{\kappa_G,\kappa_g\}$. Coverage IDs identify the exact
EDR/NDR SourceCoverage objects used by the resolver. The digest covers their RFC~8785 canonical
health snapshot.

\subsection{Deterministic Episode Synthesis and Revisions}
\label{sec:episode-synthesis}

For event $e_i$ and open episode $E$,
\begin{equation}
\ell(e_i,E)=\mathbf{1}[\Delta_t\le300\mathrm{s}\land R=1\land
\operatorname{span}(E\cup\{e_i\})\le3600\mathrm{s}\land H=0].
\label{eq:link-eligibility}
\end{equation}
$R$ comprises process-causal, authenticated-session, endpoint-mapped connection, and
specific-entity relations. Their precedence $\rho$ is respectively $4,3,2,1$; $n_R$ is the
number of distinct satisfied relation classes. $H=1$ when all support comes from an entity with
at least 25 distinct relations in the preceding 600 s. Eligible candidates use the sole order
$(-\rho,-n_R,\Delta_t,t_{start},\mathrm{episode\_id})$.

Zero/one candidates start/attach. Multiple candidates merge only when there are at most three
parents, every parent is connected through the new event by a causal, session, or endpoint
relation, and the result has at most 500 events and 200 entities; otherwise the first ordered
candidate receives the event. Start and merge IDs hash the policy version and canonical
inputs; membership order is $(t^{event},t^{ingest},\mathrm{source\_instance},
\mathrm{event\_id})$.

The watermark is maximum observed ingest time minus 120 s. When an open lineage first satisfies
the watermark, Episode Synthesis emits a \texttt{finalized} successor and freezes
$t_f$ and deadline $t_f+900$ s. The lineage leaves the open-candidate index but remains in a
separate revisable index. A closed-lineage index retains canonical relation keys and the latest
revision until deadline plus $W_{ret}=86400$ s. Before that fixed deadline, a late event is accepted only if
Equation~\ref{eq:link-eligibility} holds against the latest revision; acceptance emits a new
\texttt{finalized} successor with the unioned membership and the same $t_f$ and deadline. It
does not reopen the lineage or extend the horizon. At the deadline a membership-identical
\texttt{closed} successor is emitted, removed from the revisable index, and retained in the
closed-lineage index. A later related event emits an immutable LateEvidenceRecord with reason
\texttt{beyond\_revision\_horizon}, \texttt{closed\_lineage\_match}, or
\texttt{duplicate\_after\_terminal\_outcome} to the late store; it cannot create an EpisodeRevision or change membership, $z$, $P$, or an earlier
AuditRecord. An event older than $W_{ret}$ is rejected to RT7 as
\texttt{closed\_index\_expired}; it cannot start a fresh episode.

\paragraph{Algorithm 1: label-blind update.}
Validate and root-deduplicate the event. First query revisable and closed-lineage indexes using
event time and canonical relation keys. If any match, select one by
$(state\ priority,-\rho,-n_R,\Delta_t,t_{start},id)$ with revisable before closed; emit exactly one
accepted successor or LateEvidenceRecord and return. Only when no non-open lineage matches may
the event query sorted open candidates and execute exactly one start/attach/merge action. Events
past $W_{ret}$ fail ingress. Advance the watermark and emit each immutable state successor or
side record. Replay asserts equality of IDs, membership, links,
states, late records, features, outputs, evidence references, and canonical digests. Figure
~\ref{fig:episode-revision-lifecycle} illustrates the fixed transition points.

\subsection{Compact Features and Stateless Raw Score}
\label{sec:feature-semantics}

The primary scorer input is
\begin{equation}
x_\epsilon=[D_\epsilon,C_\epsilon].
\label{eq:feature-vector}
\end{equation}
The frozen detector registry contains exactly three EDR IDs and three NDR IDs; each emits only a
finite binary64 value $r\in[0,1]$, with larger values more suspicious. Source family, detector
ID, and dependency group form the deduplication key. Byte-identical retries collapse; a
conflicting value or reference rejects the FeatureVector. For each detector, negative training revisions define
$\widehat F(r)=(\#\{v<r\}+0.5\#\{v=r\})/N$; $N=0$ invalidates the registry. Sort normalized
values descending, breaking ties by UTF-8 detector ID then dependency-group ID. If $n>0$, $D$ is
the mean of the first $\min(3,n)$ values; if $n=0$, $D=0$ is a diagnostic placeholder and the
revision is not decision eligible. Missing/nonfinite/out-of-range values are never zero-imputed.
For source $s$, $q_{\epsilon,s}=1$ iff at least one root-deduplicated registered detector has
\texttt{detected}, a legal value, and a root tied to a core entity or selected edge; otherwise it
is zero. Thus corroboration is fixed to
\begin{equation}
C_\epsilon=q_{\epsilon,\mathrm{EDR}}q_{\epsilon,\mathrm{NDR}},\qquad K=2.
\label{eq:corroboration}
\end{equation}
Identity is only an offline M5-ID annotation and cannot alter candidates or features.

With $\lambda=10^{-4}$, the stateless scorer is the unique frozen solution
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
(\hat b,\hat w_D,\hat w_C)&=\arg\min_{b\in\mathbb R,\;w_D,w_C\ge0}
 \frac1N\sum_i[\log(1+e^{z_i})-y_i z_i]\\
&\quad+\frac\lambda2(b^2+w_D^2+w_C^2),\\
z_i&=b+w_DD_i+w_CC_i.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:raw-logit}
\end{equation}
Its cohort is the first finalized revision per training lineage with exact EDR/NDR health/parser
support and an offline positive/negative label; mixed, ambiguous, OOS, and failed outcomes are
excluded and counted. Deterministic binary64 L-BFGS-B starts at zero, uses stable
\texttt{logaddexp}, stops at projected-gradient infinity norm $\le10^{-9}$ and objective change
$\le10^{-12}$, and fails closed after 10,000 iterations. The RFC~8785/SHA-256 model artifact
covers ordered training revision IDs/labels, detector-registry and normalizer digests, matrix
bytes, $\lambda$, solver contract, and final coefficients. Health, $m$, $a^{det}$, $\kappa$, $U$,
and outage status never enter $x_\epsilon$.

\subsection{Manifest-Scoped Affine Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

The global calibrator uniquely minimizes natural-prevalence calibration NLL plus
$10^{-6}(a^2+b_{cal}^2)$ subject to $a\ge10^{-6}$. Thus
\begin{equation}
P_\epsilon=
\begin{cases}
\sigma(a z_\epsilon+b_{cal}) & e=1,\ \kappa=\kappa_G,\\
\sigma(a_g z_\epsilon+b_g) & e=1,\ \kappa=\kappa_g,\\
\texttt{null} & e=0,\ \kappa=\kappa_\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:affine-calibration}
\end{equation}
following positive-slope score calibration~\cite{platt1999probabilistic,gneiting2007proper}.
The primary manifest supports only exact EDR+NDR mask \texttt{11}, non-down/non-unknown health,
valid parsers, resolved detector states \texttt{available} or \texttt{no\_detection}, and exact
pipeline versions. Any mismatch is out-of-support.

Before calibration, the manifest registers each group key as the RFC~8785/SHA-256 digest of a
canonical object containing deployment profile and EDR/NDR detector versions; scenario, run,
identity, and label fields are forbidden.
Conditional groups use deterministic balanced five-fold run-level cross-fitting on the
calibration partition. Within each group, intact runs with binary counts $(p_r,n_r)$ sort by
decreasing $p_r+n_r$, then seeded SHA-256 and run ID. Each run goes to the fold minimizing the
resulting maximum normalized positive, negative, and run loads; ties use total normalized load
then fold ID. Every fold must be nonempty, and the ordered assignment map is sealed by digest.
A group requires at least 50 positive revisions, 50 negatives, and 10 runs. For each held-out fold, improvement is
$\Delta B=\operatorname{Brier}_{global}-\operatorname{Brier}_{group}$ and
$\Delta N=\operatorname{NLL}_{global}-\operatorname{NLL}_{group}$, first macro-averaged by run
and then equally across folds. Both means must be at least 0.01, every fold must be nonnegative,
and paired run-bootstrap lower 95\% bounds for both must exceed zero. A passing group is
refit once on all calibration-partition records with that key; its final coefficients and key are
sealed in the manifest. Manifest validation rejects duplicate keys. Runtime resolution is
exclusive: first reject any support mismatch; otherwise select the single enabled exact key, or
fall back to global when none is enabled. Scope Resolver alone emits $g$, $\kappa$, and
$e=\mathbf{1}[n>0\land\kappa\ne\kappa_\varnothing]$; Calibrator only maps $(z,e,\kappa,g)$ to
$P$. This invariant prevents \path{no_detection} health support from making an empty detector
vector score eligible.

\subsection{Structured Sufficiency and Audit Assembly}
\label{sec:sufficiency}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
U_\epsilon=(&u_{coverage},u_{provenance},u_{lateness},\\
             &u_{contradiction},u_{calibration}).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sufficiency}
\end{equation}

Table~\ref{tab:sufficiency} makes every $U$ axis a first-match deterministic rule rather than an
informal confidence judgment.

The frozen contradiction rules are CR1, overlapping authenticated endpoint bindings map one
endpoint key to different hosts; CR2, matched EDR socket and NDR flow disagree on the initiator
after canonical direction normalization; and CR3, the same $(host,boot,pid,start)$ process key
has conflicting parent/root lineage. A rule is applicable only when its relation exists; absent
required fields then yield \texttt{unevaluable}. No applicable relation yields \texttt{none}.
$U$ is never scalarized and cannot gate, modify, or relabel $P$.

AuditRecord Assembler joins one EpisodeRevision, FeatureVector, $z$, SupportDecision, $P$, $U$,
and the exact SourceCoverage objects referenced by SupportDecision, by
$(episode\_id,revision)$ and exact versions. It copies the health snapshot from those references;
it cannot independently select or infer health. Required provenance comprises every membership
event/root, selected link, scored input, coverage object, and version used by $z,P,U$; only
unscored diagnostic references are optional. Identical retries are idempotent; conflicting
duplicates fail. At deterministic deadline $\texttt{revision.watermark}+120$ s it atomically inserts
exactly one AssemblyOutcome under that key: a complete AuditRecord, or an AssemblyFailureRecord
with one code from \{\texttt{assembly\_timeout}, \texttt{missing\_input},
\texttt{conflicting\_duplicate}, \texttt{key\_mismatch}, \texttt{version\_mismatch},
\texttt{schema\_invalid}, \texttt{digest\_mismatch}\} and no fabricated score/probability fields.
The unique-key store grants
insert/select only and forbids a later success after terminal failure. RT1/RT7 count failures.
Inputs are processed in fixed type order EpisodeRevision, FeatureVector, raw logit,
SupportDecision, $P$, $U$, SourceCoverage. When multiple failures apply, precedence is schema,
key, duplicate, digest, version, missing-input, then timeout. Before canonicalization, references
sort by relation rank (membership/link/feature/health/contradiction), dependency-group UTF-8
bytes, object reference before sentinel, object-ID-or-empty bytes, then digest-or-empty bytes.
RFC~8785 preserves JSON array element order rather than sorting it; JCS is applied only after
this application-level ordering. Optional absence uses a closed sentinel; missing required
provenance fails assembly. Every closed VersionSet component binds a version identifier and
SHA-256 digest for schema/parser/formation, detector registry, feature, normalizer, raw model,
calibrator, support, sufficiency, assembly, and manifest. JSON numbers must be finite binary64 values represented by JSON
decimal grammar; NaN, infinities, negative zero, and overflow fail schema validation. Runtime
host, PID, wall-clock, and duration form a non-canonical envelope excluded from digests.

\subsection{Boundary and Reproducibility Contract}
\label{sec:method-boundary}

The method excludes CTI, RAG/agents, impact/priority, automated response, Identity as an online
source, and OT/ICS generalization. Appendix~\ref{app:data-contracts} and the tables cited in the
main text are normative. The required supplementary
\path{TrustEpisode_reproducibility_companion_v1.zip} contains the executable JSON Schema and
registries; its SHA-256 is
\texttt{6298bd2e89bd50a7\allowbreak{}8a890b62750dcca2\allowbreak{}bcee34906fd6dd73\allowbreak{}a4a77f2a19c81205}. A missing or
mismatched upload makes the machine-contract claim unverified. The contribution is limited to
deterministic EDR/NDR episode construction, manifest-scoped probability, structured sufficiency,
and replayable append-only provenance.

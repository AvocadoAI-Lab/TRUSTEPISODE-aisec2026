\section{TrustEpisode Method}
\label{sec:method}

\trustepisode transforms endpoint detection and response (EDR) and network detection and response (NDR) telemetry into deterministic candidate-episode revisions and version-scoped evidence records. The online path is label blind: the Evidence Fabric normalizes events and collector-health observations; Episode Synthesis alone owns event membership and immutable revisions; a stateless scorer maps two explicit evidence features to a raw logit; and an affine calibrator is the sole producer of a maliciousness probability when the current revision matches a supported manifest. A separate deterministic evaluator reports a structured evidence-sufficiency profile without changing that probability. Each revision produces exactly one append-only AuditRecord containing the raw score, nullable calibrated probability, sufficiency state, versions, provenance references, and canonical digest. Ground truth is joined only after inference for fitting or evaluation. The method therefore estimates whether a frozen candidate revision contains malicious activity under the observed EDR/NDR collection boundary; it does not estimate enterprise-wide risk, operational impact, response priority, or attacks that leave no observable trace. Figure~\ref{fig:method-architecture} summarizes these ownership and trust boundaries.

\subsection{Problem Scope, Output Contract, and Ownership}
\label{sec:problem-formulation}

Let $\mathcal C$ be every EpisodeRevision emitted by the frozen formation policy. Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sup}}=
\{\epsilon^{(r)}\in\mathcal C:
\kappa_{\epsilon^{(r)}}\in
\{\kappa_G,\kappa_g\}\}
\label{eq:supported-population}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_G$ and $\kappa_g$ denote \path{supported_global} and
\path{supported_group}. This is the subset for which a calibration manifest supports a
probability. The supervised target
$Y_{\epsilon^{(r)}}\in\{0,1\}$ states whether the immutable membership of revision
$\epsilon^{(r)}$ contains malicious activity under a frozen temporal and entity-overlap policy.
The label is joined offline only after that revision has completed online inference. A malicious
action first observed in revision $r+1$ cannot retroactively relabel revision $r$. Positive and
negative revisions form the primary supervised cohort; mixed and ambiguous revisions are
reported separately and enter only pre-registered sensitivity analyses.

For every $\epsilon^{(r)}\in\mathcal C$, runtime emits a raw logit $z_{\epsilon^{(r)}}$, a
calibration status $\kappa_{\epsilon^{(r)}}$, a nullable calibrated probability
$P_{\epsilon^{(r)}}$, a structured sufficiency profile $U_{\epsilon^{(r)}}$, and exactly one
immutable AuditRecord. When defined, $P$ is candidate-conditioned and version-scoped:
\begin{equation}
P_{\epsilon^{(r)}} =
\Pr(Y_{\epsilon^{(r)}}=1\mid
\epsilon^{(r)},v_{\mathrm{episode}},v_{\mathrm{feature}},
v_{\mathrm{model}},v_{\mathrm{cal}}).
\label{eq:scoped-probability}
\end{equation}
It is not an unconditional probability that the organization is under attack.

The contract relies on three assumptions. The \emph{observable-trace assumption} limits
inference to traces visible inside the declared EDR/NDR scope. The
\emph{collector-boundary assumption} treats collector identity and transport metadata as
authenticated while representing outages, delay, and parser failure explicitly. The
\emph{label-blind online assumption} forbids labels, scenario identifiers, split assignments,
and ground-truth windows from every online object. Consequently, \trustepisode cannot
guarantee detection of activity that leaves no relevant observable trace.

\subsection{Evidence Fabric and Independent Source Health}
\label{sec:evidence-fabric}

The Evidence Fabric maps each source record to a NormalizedEvent containing a stable ID,
event time, ingest time, source family and instance, canonical entities and action, an
immutable raw pointer, and a root dependency group. Event time describes when the source
observed an action; ingest time describes when the pipeline received it. The distinction is
required for deterministic late-data handling~\cite{akidau2015dataflow}. Forwarded or
derived copies inherit the same root dependency group and cannot create additional evidence.
Normalization artifacts, including source-specific parsers and empirical transforms, are fitted
or selected using training data and are read-only at runtime. OCSF v1.8.0 provides a field-name
alignment target, not a claim of cross-vendor semantic equivalence~\cite{ocsf2026}.

SourceCoverage is a separate health stream produced by authenticated heartbeat, lag, parser,
and transport observations. Absence of an event never implies that a collector is down. For
source family $s\in\mathcal S^\star=\{\mathrm{EDR},\mathrm{NDR}\}$, define
\begin{equation}
m_{\epsilon,s}=\mathbb{1}[
\epsilon\text{ contains an observed event from }s].
\label{eq:source-mask}
\end{equation}
The vector $m_\epsilon$ is the observed source mask; replay event masking can change $m$
without changing health. The snapshot $h_\epsilon$ is resolved from SourceCoverage intervals
that overlap the episode interval. Finally, $q_{\epsilon,s}=1$ only when $s$ contains
lineage-deduplicated supporting detection evidence tied to an episode core entity or edge.
Thus a healthy but silent family can have $m=0$ and health \texttt{healthy}; an observed
family without supporting detection can have $m=1,q=0$. Neither $m$ nor $q$ alone diagnoses
collector failure. The Scope Resolver jointly uses $(m_\epsilon,h_\epsilon)$ and frozen
pipeline versions to determine $\kappa_\epsilon$. Appendix~\ref{app:data-contracts} gives the
contract-complete schema registry, canonical serialization, and firewall rules.

\subsection{Deterministic Episode Synthesis and Immutable Revisions}
\label{sec:episode-synthesis}

Episode Synthesis is the only component that may attach, merge, or revise membership. For a
new event $e_i$ and an open episode $E$, eligibility is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\ell(e_i,E)=\mathbb{1}\!\big[
&\Delta_t(e_i,E)\le W_{\mathrm{gap}}
\land R(e_i,E)=1\\
&{}\land \operatorname{span}(E\cup\{e_i\})\le W_{\max}
\land H(e_i,E)=0
\big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:link-eligibility}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_t$ is the event-time inactivity gap, $R$ denotes at least one frozen
process-causal, authenticated-session, endpoint-mapped connection, or specific-entity
relation, and $H$ marks a forbidden hub-only link. Temporal proximity alone is insufficient.

Zero eligible candidates start a new episode; one candidate receives the event; multiple
candidates are processed by the frozen total order and merge guard in
Appendix~\ref{app:episode-algorithm}. The appendix also fixes canonical episode IDs, merge
lineage, inclusive boundaries, and event ordering. Event time determines relation
eligibility, whereas ingest time advances the watermark. Accepted evidence inside the
revision horizon emits revision $r+1$ and a new AuditRecord; evidence beyond the horizon
follows the frozen late-store or drop policy. No path overwrites an earlier revision or its
record. These controls apply the causal-context lessons of alert-correlation and provenance
systems while retaining an auditable, deliberately compact candidate generator
~\cite{ning2002attackscenarios,holmes2019,nodlink2024,kairos2024}.

\paragraph{Algorithm 1: label-blind episode update.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Normalize and dependency-deduplicate the incoming event.
\item Retrieve open candidates satisfying the inclusive temporal bounds.
\item Evaluate allowed non-temporal relations and forbidden hub links.
\item Apply giant-component and merge guards from the frozen policy.
\item Follow the frozen zero/one/multiple-candidate branch and total order.
\item Advance the ingest-time watermark and apply the revision-horizon rule.
\item Emit an immutable EpisodeRevision and exactly one new AuditRecord.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Compact Feature Semantics and Stateless Raw Scorer}
\label{sec:feature-semantics}

The primary feature contract is
\begin{equation}
x_\epsilon=[D_\epsilon,C_\epsilon].
\label{eq:feature-vector}
\end{equation}
For each eligible source-native detector value $r_i$, the frozen transform
$d_i=\widehat F^{\mathrm{train,benign}}_{s_i}(r_i)$ maps the value through the empirical CDF
fitted on training-benign observations from its source family. $D_\epsilon$ is the mean of the
three largest eligible $d_i$ values, or all values when fewer than three exist. If none exist,
$D_\epsilon=0$ is only a deterministic raw-model placeholder and
$u_{\mathrm{provenance}}=\texttt{missing}$. This is not benign evidence; unless the
calibration manifest explicitly supports that regime, $P_\epsilon=\mathrm{NA}$.

The primary scope is permanently fixed to
\begin{equation}
\mathcal S^\star=\{\mathrm{EDR},\mathrm{NDR}\},\qquad K=2,
\end{equation}
and corroboration is
\begin{equation}
C_\epsilon =
q_{\epsilon,\mathrm{EDR}}q_{\epsilon,\mathrm{NDR}}.
\label{eq:corroboration}
\end{equation}
Heartbeat, inventory, and dependency-linked forwarded copies do not set $q=1$. Identity, if
available in a later pilot-gated study, remains an offline exploratory annotation: it does not
enter NormalizedEvent, Episode Synthesis, candidate IDs or membership, $D$, $C$, any
calibrator manifest, or a primary research question. Thus it cannot change the candidate
universe, and $\mathcal S^\star$ and $K=2$ remain fixed.

The stateless scorer is
\begin{equation}
z_\epsilon=b+w_DD_\epsilon+w_CC_\epsilon,
\qquad w_D,w_C\ge0.
\label{eq:raw-logit}
\end{equation}
Its only learned coefficients are $(b,w_D,w_C)$. Training minimizes unweighted
candidate-level log loss; formation and feature settings are selected using training and
development data, then frozen before the final train-only normalizer and scorer are fitted.
Campaign/run is the split and uncertainty cluster. Health, $m$, $\kappa$, $U$, labels, and
outage status never enter $x_\epsilon$. The terms $w_DD_\epsilon$ and $w_CC_\epsilon$ are
replayable logit decompositions, not causal explanations. Appendix~\ref{app:parameter-registry}
records preprocessing artifacts, implementation settings, owners, and freeze points.

\subsection{Manifest-Scoped Affine Calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

Neither $z_\epsilon$ nor $\sigma(z_\epsilon)$ is called a validated probability. The primary
calibrator is a positive-slope global affine mapping fitted on an independent calibration
split with natural candidate prevalence, following the score-to-probability principle of
Platt scaling and evaluation with proper scoring rules
~\cite{platt1999probabilistic,gneiting2007proper}:
\begin{equation}
P_\epsilon=
\begin{cases}
\sigma(a z_\epsilon+b_{\mathrm{cal}}),
& \kappa_\epsilon=\kappa_G,\\
\sigma(a_g z_\epsilon+b_g),
& \kappa_\epsilon=\kappa_g,\\
\mathrm{NA},
& \kappa_\epsilon=\kappa_\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\quad a,a_g>0.
\label{eq:affine-calibration}
\end{equation}
$\kappa_\varnothing$ denotes \path{out_of_support}; $\kappa_G$ and $\kappa_g$ retain the
status meanings defined after Equation~\ref{eq:supported-population}.
The global mapping preserves ranking over the same candidate set. A group mapping preserves
ranking only within that group and makes no cross-group ranking claim. Group calibration is
conditional: a pre-registered gate requires sufficient positive revisions, negative revisions,
and independent runs plus evidence that the global mapping is inadequate. Selection and fit
use run-level cross-fitting or an internal calibration holdout, never the locked test.

Exact support requires the current episode, feature, detector, raw-model, label-policy,
candidate-selection, and schema versions plus source mask to match the calibrator manifest.
A supported group uses \path{supported_group}; a supported revision that fails only the
group gate falls back to the primary global mapping as \path{supported_global}; a manifest
or regime mismatch is \path{out_of_support}, stores canonical null for $P$, and sets
\path{decision_eligible=false}. It retains $z$ for diagnosis but no unvalidated number in
$[0,1]$ is named a probability. Appendix~\ref{app:calibration-manifest} defines the resolver,
manifest, gate, and invalidation matrix.

\subsection{Independent Structured Sufficiency and Append-Only Audit}
\label{sec:sufficiency}

Evidence sufficiency is a structured state:
\begin{equation}
U_\epsilon=(u_{\mathrm{coverage}},u_{\mathrm{provenance}},
u_{\mathrm{lateness}},u_{\mathrm{contradiction}},
u_{\mathrm{calibration}}).
\label{eq:sufficiency}
\end{equation}

$U$ is not scalar confidence: its axes are never weighted, averaged, or one-hot ranked. It does
not modify, gate, or relabel $P$. The contradiction axis reads only online evidence and cannot
read scenario, ability, or label metadata. A downstream review or abstention policy may
jointly consume $P$ and $U$, but that policy is outside the method.

Each EpisodeRevision produces exactly one insert-only AuditRecord containing ordered
membership and link reasons, $D$, $C$, $z$, nullable $P$, $\kappa$, $U$, source-health
snapshot, evidence references, policy/model/calibrator versions, and a canonical digest.
Accepted late evidence creates a complete new record in the revision chain. Canonical null,
serialization, key, and hashing rules are specified in Appendix~\ref{app:data-contracts}.

\subsection{Method Boundary and Reproducibility Contract}
\label{sec:method-boundary}

The method excludes CTI enrichment, RAG or agent components, impact or priority scoring,
automated response, Identity as a primary source, OT/ICS generalization, ground-truth-assisted
online inference, and downstream queue optimization. Its canonical replay payload excludes
wall-clock execution timestamps and machine-specific runtime metadata. The contribution is
limited to deterministic candidate-episode construction, compact version-scoped probability
calibration, independent evidence-sufficiency reporting, and replayable append-only provenance
under endpoint and network telemetry.

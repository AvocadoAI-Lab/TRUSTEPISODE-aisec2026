\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

AI-assisted SOC systems increasingly use language-model agents to gather tool outputs, correlate
alerts, and draft incident narratives~\cite{react2023,cortex2025,auditableddos2026}. Their output
can be no more reviewable than the evidence interface below it: if arrival order changes episode
membership, collector failure is encoded as benign silence, or a late event rewrites history, a
fluent explanation may cite a case state that cannot be reproduced.

Security operations and digital-forensic investigations therefore need more than alert
clustering. Multi-step attacks appear as dispersed EDR and NDR observations that become material
when linked by time, entity, session, and process relations
~\cite{alertfatigue2025,nist80086,breitinger2025sok}. Investigators need a contract that fixes who
owns episode membership, how late evidence is admitted, how collector health differs from ``no
detection,'' and how every revision leaves an independently verifiable terminal outcome
~\cite{casey2011digitalevidence,carrier2005filesystem,vanini2024timeanchors}.

Prior alert-correlation and provenance systems motivate multi-stage context using rules, graphs,
and learned representations~\cite{ning2002attackscenarios,holmes2019,unicorn2020,nodlink2024,kairos2024,captain2025}.
Those lines do not remove the need for fixed ownership of membership, immutable revision history,
and replayable audit semantics under incomplete observability. This submission evaluates
\trustepisode as a \emph{reconstruction contract that is deterministic over canonicalized,
version-bound inputs} for EDR/NDR evidence
and as a typed, read-only boundary for downstream analyst or AI reasoning. The downstream model
is deliberately outside the trusted computing base: it may summarize versioned AuditRecords but
cannot own membership, infer source health, overwrite history, or convert unsupported evidence
into a numeric probability. A compact scorer and calibrator remain optional schema interfaces and
are not empirically validated here. Thus, our AI-security claim concerns \emph{evidence
truthfulness and containment}, not broad LLM effectiveness. We additionally report a small,
held-out Qwen pilot and a post-result locked replication on the deployment-default Gemma model
to measure extraction, citation, and abstention over the typed record. They are boundary stress
tests rather than a preregistered model comparison.

\paragraph{Research questions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}] Can TrustEpisode reproduce byte-identical membership, revision history,
        late-evidence records, and digests on 60 sealed malicious runs?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}] Do open, finalized, late-successor, closed, beyond-horizon, duplicate, and
        all seven AssemblyFailureRecord codes yield explicit immutable audit outcomes without
        rewriting earlier history?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}] When a required action is absent from a reconstructed episode, can the
        pipeline localize loss across the closed A--G taxonomy, and is that taxonomy instantiated
        on sealed live evidence?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}] Which relation, hub, merge, and late-revision mechanisms does the live
        M1--M7 cohort exercise, and which require targeted conformance cards?
  \item[\textbf{RQ5}] What descriptive coverage change accompanies replacing fixed bins with
        sliding sessionization on the sealed cohort, and do fair relation-aware builders show
        additional measurable benefit?
  \item[\textbf{RQ6}] Do closed schemas and digest bindings fail closed when a downstream
        consumer injects asserted facts, labels, or unsupported probability; removes provenance;
        or tampers with revision and evidence digests; what remains possible after a schema-valid
        reseal; and how reliably do two local models follow the resulting read-only extraction,
        citation, and abstention interface?
\end{enumerate}
These questions evaluate the evidence substrate. The deployed interface trace reported later
checks end-to-end materialization but is not counted as an effectiveness trial, and its displayed
operational confidence is not the unevaluated calibrated probability field defined by the
contract.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a single-owner reconstruction and audit contract with immutable late-data semantics,
        canonical references and digests, exactly one terminal AssemblyOutcome per revision, and a
        read-only agent boundary;
  \item a sealed evaluation separating evidence classes: 70 primary malicious formation runs plus
        40 standalone benign execution controls, fair EB0--EB4 baselines, a 60-run determinism
        matrix, a matrix covering all seven predefined AssemblyFailureRecord codes, contract
        ablations, and an A--G loss taxonomy that localizes M2 to token mapping;
  \item downstream stress tests that reject six schema-invalid or unsealed mutations, deliberately
        accept a resealed allowed-field negative control to expose the writer-authorization
        dependency, and reveal extraction/citation/abstention failures in held-out Qwen and
        locked same-card Gemma audits, without claiming model superiority; and
  \item an executable anonymous companion plus a deployed read-only trace showing
        source-separated Suricata/Wazuh evidence and ATT\&CK support at the analyst surface.
\end{enumerate}

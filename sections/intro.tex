\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Multi-step attacks may not produce a single conclusive alert. A remote connection, script
execution, file transfer, or administrative action can be benign alone yet material when linked
by time, entity, session, and process relations. Endpoint detection and response (EDR) and
network detection and response (NDR) evidence may also be absent for distinct reasons: no
matching event was observed, a detector examined the evidence but returned no detection, the
evidence was not examined, or required collector/parser coverage was unavailable or unknown. Telemetry
abstraction can likewise remove evidence needed to support an inference without showing that the
underlying behavior did not occur~\cite{vaseghipanah2026blindspots}. \trustepisode therefore
preserves event observation, detector observation, and authenticated source health as separate
states rather than encoding absence as zero evidence.

Existing alert-correlation and provenance systems reconstruct causal
context~\cite{ning2002attackscenarios,holmes2019,nodlink2024,kairos2024}. To our knowledge,
prior work does not jointly specify deterministic candidate boundaries, bounded immutable
successor revisions under late evidence, manifest-scoped calibration support, and byte-identical
replay as one versioned contract. Separately, a classifier score is not automatically a
calibrated probability, and probabilistic outputs require calibration diagnostics and strictly
proper scoring rules~\cite{guo2017calibration,gneiting2007proper}. These gaps interact: changing
episode membership changes the candidate population to which a probability applies, while
zero-imputing unavailable telemetry can turn an unsupported case into an ordinary scorer input.

\trustepisode addresses this problem with a deliberately narrow, label-blind contract limited to
EDR/NDR observations. Formation, scoring, calibration, and evidence sufficiency are strictly
separated: a health-blind score drives a calibrated probability only within registered manifest
support, canonical null is emitted otherwise, and sufficiency is reported independently without
altering that probability. Each revision resolves to exactly one auditable terminal outcome,
successful or not, and ground truth is joined only after inference.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The contract is deliberately narrow: enterprise risk, response priority, CTI or agentic
reasoning, and OT/ICS generalization are out of scope, detailed fully in
Section~\ref{sec:related}. Section~4 evaluates formation, ranking/calibration, partial
telemetry, and deterministic replay without feeding labels back into the online path.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item a single-owner online object model and deterministic episode state machine with fixed
        guards, total ordering, a watermark, and a bounded immutable successor-revision policy;
  \item a compact health-blind scorer over $[D,C]$ and manifest-scoped affine calibration that
        emits probability only under registered global or group support, and null otherwise;
  \item deterministic detector-availability and five-axis sufficiency rules, plus a closed
        terminal assembly contract with explicit nullability, digest-linked provenance, and
        exactly one immutable AuditRecord or AssemblyFailureRecord per revision; and
  \item a preregistered controlled-lab protocol with fixed populations, splits, thresholds,
        perturbations, matching, statistics, and pending RT1--RT7 result schemas.
\end{enumerate}

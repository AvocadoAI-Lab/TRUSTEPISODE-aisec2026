\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Alert correlation and provenance.}
Classical alert-correlation methods connect observations into attack scenarios using temporal,
causal, and prerequisite relations~\cite{ning2002attackscenarios}. Provenance-based intrusion
detection preserves process and information-flow context; HOLMES, NODLINK, and KAIROS show how
causal graphs support multi-stage reconstruction and investigation
~\cite{holmes2019,nodlink2024,kairos2024}. CAPTAIN further demonstrates that a lightweight,
interpretable rule system can adapt deployment-specific parameters~\cite{captain2025}.
\trustepisode differs by treating deterministic cross-source candidate construction,
revision ownership, and candidate-conditioned probability calibration as one versioned
contract rather than presenting a new graph detector.

\paragraph{Calibration and incomplete telemetry.}
Ranking and calibration answer different questions: a high AUPRC does not imply reliable
probabilities~\cite{guo2017calibration,gneiting2007proper}. Platt-style affine calibration is
compact and ranking-preserving when its slope is positive, but only for a fixed candidate
population and support regime~\cite{platt1999probabilistic}. Missing-modality research likewise
shows that absent inputs require explicit handling rather than silent zero imputation
~\cite{missingmodality2024}. \trustepisode therefore separates observed source masks,
authenticated health, detector availability, calibration status, and sufficiency. The raw
scorer cannot read health or labels, and unsupported records retain a diagnostic logit but no
numeric probability.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The method does not model operational impact, response priority, autonomous response, CTI
enrichment, or language-agent behavior. Its claims are limited to deterministic EDR/NDR episode
formation, manifest-scoped maliciousness calibration, explicit evidence sufficiency, and
replayable audit records.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Alert Correlation and Attack Reconstruction}

Classical alert correlation reconstructs multi-step scenarios from intrusion alerts by temporal
and causal linking~\cite{ning2002attackscenarios}. HOLMES and UNICORN recover APT context from
system graphs~\cite{holmes2019,unicorn2020}; KAIROS, NODLINK, and CAPTAIN extend this line
~\cite{kairos2024,nodlink2024,captain2025}. These systems motivate episode construction but optimize different output units. We therefore
use them for conceptual positioning rather than as direct effectiveness baselines. \trustepisode
instead evaluates the lifecycle contract governing mixed-EDR/NDR membership, late-data revisions,
and terminal audit outcomes.

\subsection{Digital Forensic Event Reconstruction}

Timeline-based event reconstruction is a core digital-forensic technique for inferring past
activity from heterogeneous artifacts~\cite{breitinger2025sok,casey2011digitalevidence}. Recent
work formalizes timestamp interpretation via time anchors and clock-skew anomalies
~\cite{vanini2024timeanchors} and surveys challenges beyond mere data volume
~\cite{breitinger2025sok}. Process models and filesystem analysis emphasize documenting what was
examined, what was unavailable, and how conclusions depend on incomplete traces
~\cite{carrier2005filesystem,nist80086}. TrustEpisode imports these audit requirements into
\emph{online} EDR/NDR episode reconstruction: immutable revisions, explicit late stores, and
digest-bound terminal outcomes under delayed delivery.

\subsection{Reproducibility, Tool Validation, and Provenance}

Forensic tool and method validation requires documented procedures, known datasets, and
repeatable results before casework~\cite{swgde2018tooltesting,brunty2022validation,wales2026validation}.
Chain-of-custody and provenance discipline demand that investigators show integrity of examined
objects and distinguish observed from unavailable evidence~\cite{casey2011digitalevidence,nist80086}.
\trustepisode applies these reproducibility expectations through a single-owner revision contract,
a canonical replay boundary, and explicit terminal failure semantics that make reconstruction
outcomes auditable under the same reproducibility requirements.

\subsection{Incomplete Telemetry and Evidence-Loss Attribution}

Evidence loss can arise at multiple stages of an EDR/NDR pipeline, including sensor
observability, parsing or normalization, and downstream reconstruction
~\cite{missingmodality2024,vaseghipanah2026blindspots}. A missing attack step therefore
should not automatically be attributed to correlation failure. Reconstruction contracts must
distinguish upstream evidence loss from downstream attribution errors when diagnosing
incomplete episodes.

\subsection{Evidence-Grounded and Contained AI Investigation}

Tool-using language-model systems interleave reasoning with external observations
~\cite{react2023}. In security operations, CORTEX coordinates specialized agents over enterprise
evidence, while Holmes packages network evidence into auditable anchors for an LLM investigator
~\cite{cortex2025,auditableddos2026}. These systems motivate evidence-grounded AI assistance, but
grounding also needs an upstream contract: citation presence does not by itself establish
faithfulness~\cite{alce2023,ragfaithfulness2025}, and agent execution requires architectural
containment rather than prompt-only trust~\cite{isolategpt2025}. \trustepisode provides the complementary upstream layer. It defines which normalized EDR/NDR
objects are admitted to each versioned case state, which producer owns them, how missingness is
represented, and which immutable references a later narrative may cite.
We do not claim a superior LLM or better autonomous triage.

\subsection{Positioning Summary}

narrative. The novelty claim concerns the composition and joint evaluation of these properties:
a single membership writer, immutable successor and late-store semantics, source health
separated from event silence, exactly one terminal outcome per revision, stage-localized
evidence loss, and citable read-only records. Contract invariants and formation
effectiveness are evaluated separately; no new detector or classifier is claimed.

\subsection{Scope Distinction}

\trustepisode does not claim a new graph detector, AI ranking effectiveness, attribution beyond
observable EDR/NDR, enterprise impact estimation, or autonomous response. The evaluated
contribution is a deterministic forensic reconstruction and audit contract over canonicalized, version-bound
EDR/NDR inputs under late arrival and incomplete observability. Its AISec relevance is architectural:
AI assistance is constrained to read and cite versioned case facts rather than silently redefining
them.

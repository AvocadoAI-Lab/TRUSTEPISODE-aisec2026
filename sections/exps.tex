\label{sec:research-questions}

\begin{itemize}

  \item \textbf{RQ6} downstream boundary and reading --- synthetic AI0--AI7 canonical-record

\subsection{Ground Truth and Matching}
\label{sec:ground-truth}

Offline matching uses temporal IoU $\ge0.10$, entity Jaccard $\ge0.50$, overlapping required
action tokens, and weight
\begin{equation}
w=0.5A_{cov}+0.3J_{entity}+0.2\operatorname{IoU}_{time};
\quad w\ge\theta_{match}=0.50.
\label{eq:match-weight}
\end{equation}
Exact-complete recovery requires $A_{cov}=1$. Online builders never read evaluation fields.

The fair sliding baselines are not identical. EB4 matches EB2 on all action-coverage pairs
(\EbTwoActionWTL{} W/T/L). Against EB1, action coverage and exact-complete recovery have
\EbOneActionWTL{} W/T/L, mean $\Delta=\EbOneActionDelta{}$, and 95\% CI
$[\EbOneActionCILo{},\EbOneActionCIHi{}]$; episode-F1 has mean $\Delta=-0.010$ and 95\% CI
$[-0.029,0]$. The single loss is the concurrent-benign M7 run
\texttt{run-ee27d58be2db032c44d0774d9358c4b1}: EB1 recovers its truth group while EB4 does not.
We therefore report the EB4--EB1 result as \emph{not statistically resolved}, not ``no observed
difference,'' equivalence, or relation-aware superiority. EB3 shows the same one-run
action-coverage pattern.

\paragraph{Why sliding-time already suffices on M1--M7.}
The sealed primary cohort does not make advanced relations the unique linkage path: EB4 and EB2
match on all 70 action-coverage pairs, and EB4 matches EB1 on 69. On the remaining
concurrent-benign M7 run, EB1 and EB3 recover a truth group that EB4 and EB2 do not. Thus the
advanced-guard policy incurs one observed boundary-case loss in this cohort rather than an
end-to-end gain. End-to-end F1 alone can misleadingly suggest that complex policy is ``validated''
when the benchmark rarely stresses it. Future BAS cohorts should register mechanism-coverage
requirements (session-only, endpoint-only, hub over-merge, late horizon, beyond-$W_{max}$ span)
before claiming relation-aware gains. M7 intentionally places the final action at 3900\,s,
outside the frozen $W_{max}=3600$\,s span; production EB4's zero coverage/F1 is the expected
boundary-stress outcome rather than a collection failure.

\subsection{Held-Out Local-Model Grounding Audits}
\label{sec:llm-grounding-pilot}

\input{results/tables/tab_llm_grounding}

After a separate unscored 18-card interface smoke test, we froze the system prompt, output schema,
four newly valued and re-digested schema-valid AuditRecords, and 20 held-out tasks. We then ran
\texttt{qwen2.5:7b} (model digest prefix \texttt{845dbda0ea48}, Ollama 0.20.5) once with
temperature 0 and seed 20260723. Tasks require exact scalar extraction, null/missing-field
abstention, or resistance to a task instruction that supplies a false value. The frozen artifact
retains prompts, raw responses, token counts, card/record/prompt digests, and per-field scoring.

After observing Qwen, but before downloading or querying the deployment configuration's default
\texttt{gemma3:1b}, we timestamped a locked replication protocol fixing the same prompt, schema,
cards, records, seed, and scoring. Gemma (digest prefix \texttt{8648f39daa8f}) then ran once.
Because this replication was registered after the Qwen result, Table~\ref{tab:llm-grounding} is
an exploratory transfer audit, not a preregistered or statistical model comparison.

Qwen yields \LlmPilotAnswers{}/20 exact answers, \LlmPilotCitations{}/20 exact citation sets,
and \LlmPilotExact{}/20 fully exact responses; one pointer is invalid. Gemma yields only
\GemmaPilotJson{}/20 schema-shaped responses, \GemmaPilotAnswers{}/20 exact answers,
\GemmaPilotCitations{}/20 exact citation sets, and \GemmaPilotExact{}/20 fully exact responses,
with \GemmaPilotInvalidRefs{} invalid pointers. Its raw H17, H18, and H20 responses echo injected
numeric or object fragments; this lexical observation was not a frozen metric and is illustrative.
The observed cross-model variability is the point of the boundary: typed input makes failure
measurable, but does not make a model accurate. Neither run is field effectiveness,
authorization testing, or evidence for general LLM safety.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Two Ubuntu targets; non-commercial EDR; $n=5$ per cell; controlled benign workloads; standalone
benign controls are not false-positive trials; no
production base rates; fair sliding results largely overlap, but EB4 trails EB1/EB3 on one
concurrent-benign M7 run; synthetic cards $\neq$ field validation; raw order not robust; no
fitted scorer/calibration; no partial-telemetry/outage; M2 mapping not corrected in the primary
cohort. Organic hub stress remains unobserved on live streams. Wall-clock
\emph{within}-horizon holds are executed (2/2); wall-clock
\emph{beyond}-horizon LateEvidenceRecord under organic telemetry was attempted (2 sealed) but
not observed. L3 hub divergence and L4 beyond-horizon localization use controlled inject on live
seals where noted. M7 is primary boundary stress and should not be interpreted as successful
3900\,s recovery under a 3600\,s span. AI0--AI7 separate record validation from writer
authorization: AI7 confirms that a schema-valid reseal is accepted, while deployed
service-identity enforcement remains unevaluated. The LLM audits are synthetic, two-model,
same-card, and once-run;
the Gemma replication was locked only after Qwen was observed. Their 9/20 and 0/20 exact rates
cannot establish general factuality, a size effect, robustness, or operational utility.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The evaluation is a frozen, pre-registered contract rather than an open-ended list of
experiments. Dataset roles, campaign-level partitions, artifacts, support gates, interventions,
metrics, baselines, matching rules, and resampling units are fixed before the locked test is
opened. Result cells remain pending until generated from measured runs; no synthetic
performance value, curve, or confidence interval is used.

\subsection{Research Questions and Claim Boundaries}
\label{sec:research-questions}

\textbf{RQ1 -- episode formation fidelity.} Does the frozen label-blind builder recover a
larger fraction of malicious actions and paths than fixed-window and time-plus-entity
comparators while reducing fragmentation, over-merge, and benign contamination?

\textbf{RQ2 -- ranking and probability calibration.} Does the compact $D+C$ scorer improve
AUPRC and fixed-budget recall over the registered severity comparator and $D$-only ablation?
Does independent affine calibration improve Brier score, negative log-likelihood (NLL), and
reliability? A positive-slope global mapping must leave AUPRC and AUROC unchanged on the same
candidate set; a difference triggers an implementation audit. Conditional group mappings are
checked only for within-group ranking and do not claim cross-group invariance.

\textbf{RQ3 -- partial telemetry and revision behavior.} Under paired replay perturbations and
a separate physical-collector outage procedure, how do formation, $z$, supported calibration,
$U$, revisions, support status, and decision eligibility change?

\textbf{RQ4 -- reproducibility and cost.} Do frozen inputs, ordering policy, and versions
produce byte-identical canonical outputs? We measure throughput, p50/p95/p99 latency, peak
memory, storage per event and revision, and replay time without defining an unmeasured
\emph{acceptable cost} threshold.

\subsection{Ground Truth Firewall and Label Registry}
\label{sec:ground-truth}

Online schemas use explicit allowlists and deny evaluation-only fields. The firewall rejects:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \path{operation_id}, \path{ability_id}, and \path{adversary_profile};
\item \path{technique_label}, attack marker, and attack window;
\item \path{scenario_class} and \path{malicious_label}; and
\item \path{split_assignment}.
\end{itemize}
These fields cannot enter
NormalizedEvent, SourceCoverage, EpisodeRevision, FeatureVector, AuditRecord, or any online
lookup. This boundary addresses target leakage at both schema and partition levels
~\cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

The versioned label registry freezes temporal tolerance, entity-overlap rule, minimum
malicious overlap, benign-contamination threshold, adjudication procedure, exclusions, and
sensitivity analyses. A \emph{positive} revision passes the frozen malicious-overlap rule; a
\emph{negative} revision is attributable to benign/background activity and does not overlap
an attack action within the frozen tolerances. A \emph{mixed} revision exceeds both malicious
and unrelated-benign overlap thresholds; an \emph{ambiguous} revision cannot be uniquely
adjudicated because of missing or conflicting time/entity evidence. Primary binary fitting and
metrics use positive and negative revisions only; mixed and ambiguous counts are always
reported and enter the pre-registered sensitivity view. Test labels cannot select formation,
features, groups, calibration mappings, thresholds, or bins.

\subsection{Campaign-Level Splits and Artifact Freeze}
\label{sec:splits}

The independent partition unit is a complete campaign/run. All events, episodes, revisions,
paired replays, and paired outage records from a base run remain in one mutually exclusive
partition and one bootstrap cluster.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item Engineering smoke runs verify schemas, clocks, collection, and raw pointers; they never enter formal results.
\item Pilot runs estimate candidate prevalence, episode size, source-mask support, and perturbation feasibility.
\item Training fits each candidate normalizer and scorer; development selects formation, feature, and implementation settings.
\item After those choices freeze, final train-only normalization and scorer artifacts are produced.
\item The natural-prevalence calibration split fits the affine mapping and executes the pre-registered group gate.
\item The locked test is evaluated once with all artifacts, bins, metrics, and analysis scripts frozen.
\item A method change invalidates affected calibrators and requires a separately identified confirmatory test.
\end{enumerate}

No event- or episode-level random split is permitted. Calibration selects no feature, and the
locked test selects no threshold, group, fallback, bin, baseline, or metric.

\subsection{Calibration Protocol and Support Gate}
\label{sec:calibration-protocol}

Calibration has three separate procedures. First, training fits the constrained raw scorer,
and development freezes its contract. Second, the natural-prevalence calibration split fits
the primary global affine mapping. Third, the manifest resolver assigns
\texttt{supported\_global}, \texttt{supported\_group}, or
\texttt{out\_of\_support}. Conditional group selection and fitting use the internal run-level
procedure in Appendix~\ref{app:calibration-manifest}; the locked test never chooses a group.

Every supported calibration result reports candidates, positives, negatives, independent
runs, prevalence, uncalibrated-sigmoid and calibrated Brier/NLL, a reliability diagram with
bin counts, and campaign-level 95\% intervals. AUPRC and AUROC measure ranking, not
calibration. Unsupported records report out-of-support rate, supported decision coverage,
raw-logit distribution, $U$ state, and error categories; they are excluded from calibrated
Brier, NLL, and reliability calculations. CORP-style decomposition is exploratory and
reported only in the companion reproducibility appendix.

\subsection{Paired Replay Perturbations and Physical Outage}
\label{sec:partial-telemetry}

Each frozen M1--M7 base run has paired replay children for source-event masking with unchanged
health, random event drop, bounded burst drop, ingest-time delay, arrival reorder, event-time
clock skew, dependency-aware duplicate injection, and an unchanged replay control. Each child
retains its base label, split, and bootstrap cluster; variants do not increase the independent
sample size.

Physical outage is a separate procedure. A collector is actually stopped or isolated;
authenticated heartbeat transitions to degraded/down; SourceCoverage changes; and the
pipeline is observed through watermark, revision, scope-status, eligibility, recovery, and
stabilization behavior. Every outage run has a \path{pair_id} linking it to a normal run with
the same scenario and seed. Paired records remain in one split and bootstrap cluster. Replay
and outage outcomes are never pooled. All perturbation levels and outage durations are frozen
before locked test access.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:metrics}

RQ1 primary outcomes are malicious-action coverage, fragmentation rate, and over-merge rate.
Benign contamination and episode precision/recall/F1 under the frozen matching rule are
secondary. RQ2 ranking outcomes are AUPRC and recall at the fixed analyst-review budget;
Brier, NLL, and reliability are the calibration outcomes. AUPRC is emphasized for the
imbalanced candidate population~\cite{saito2015pr}. AUROC and precision at $k$ are secondary.

RQ3 has two non-pooled views. The replay table reports paired formation changes, $z$ shift,
supported-regime Brier/NLL degradation, supported decision coverage, every $U$ axis, revision
count, and stabilization. The physical-outage table reports health-detection latency,
SourceCoverage transitions, decision eligibility, recovery, and stabilization; out-of-support
records receive no calibrated proper score. RQ4 checks equality of the canonical replay hash
and reports throughput, run-level p50/p95/p99 latency, peak memory, storage per event/revision,
and replay time. Hardware, concurrency, warm-up, and measurement interval are frozen.

Campaign/run is the only resampling unit. Confidence intervals use a cluster bootstrap by run
~\cite{field2007bootstrap}; replay and outage contrasts are paired by base run. We report
absolute differences with 95\% intervals and treat relative differences as supplemental.
Every subgroup reports runs, candidates, positives, negatives, and prevalence. Event latency
is summarized inside each run before run-level aggregation. $U$ is never scalarized and no
single \emph{U-AUC} is computed. Appendix~\ref{app:experimental-contract} gives formulas,
matching, bootstrap seed, and the full RQ matrix.

\subsection{Limitations and Safety}
\label{sec:discussion}

The controlled laboratory does not represent every deployment, evasion strategy, or base
rate. DARPA TC supports only the formation claim. Calibration applies only to the frozen
manifest and explicitly supported regimes. Identity remains offline and exploratory even if
its pilot gate passes; it cannot alter primary candidates or outputs. Uncollected field data
are not part of the present evaluation. Emulation uses isolated
hosts, test accounts, harmless markers, cleanup manifests, and recorded failed or aborted
runs. The evaluation does not study CTI, agent components, impact, priority, or autonomous
response.
